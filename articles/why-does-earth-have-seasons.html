Why Does Earth Have Seasons?

During the year, many places experience changes in temperature and daylight. These seasonal changes happen mainly because Earth's axis is tilted as our planet travels around the Sun.

1. What Causes Seasons?

Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.5° from the perpendicular to its orbital plane.

As Earth completes one revolution around the Sun in about a year, the axis keeps pointing in nearly the same direction in space.

This means each hemisphere takes turns leaning towards or away from the Sun.

2. How Does Earth's Tilt Affect Seasons?

When a hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, it receives more direct sunlight and has longer days. It experiences summer.

At the same time, the opposite hemisphere is tilted away, receives less direct sunlight and has shorter days, so it experiences winter.

About six months later, the situation is reversed. Seasons are not mainly caused by Earth's changing distance from the Sun.

3. What Are the Four Seasons?

  • Spring: A transition period when temperatures generally become milder.
  • Summer: The season with longer daylight and more direct sunlight in that hemisphere.
  • Autumn (Fall): A transition period when daylight generally becomes shorter.
  • Winter: The season with shorter daylight and less direct sunlight in that hemisphere.

These four-season descriptions are most familiar in many temperate regions. Some parts of the world, including much of India, commonly describe seasons using local climate patterns such as summer, monsoon and winter.

💡 Did You Know?

Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January and farthest from it in early July.

This does not cause the seasons: the tilt of Earth's axis is the main reason. When it is winter in the Northern Hemisphere, it is summer in the Southern Hemisphere.

What Did We Learn?

Earth has seasons because its axis is tilted as it revolves around the Sun. The tilt changes the angle and duration of sunlight received by each hemisphere during the year.
