Why Do We Get Hiccups?

Almost everyone gets hiccups from time to time. They can appear suddenly, make a small "hic" sound and then disappear. But what is actually happening inside our body?

1. What Causes Hiccups?

Hiccups begin when the diaphragm — the dome-shaped muscle below the lungs that helps us breathe — suddenly contracts without us choosing to move it.

Air rushes into the lungs, and the vocal cords briefly close. This closure produces the familiar "hic" sound.

2. What Can Trigger Hiccups?

Sometimes the nerves that control the diaphragm are irritated. Common triggers include:

  • Eating too quickly or eating a very large meal.
  • Drinking fizzy beverages.
  • Swallowing extra air while eating or drinking.
  • Sudden changes in stomach temperature or excitement.

💡 Did You Know?

Most short bouts of hiccups are harmless and go away on their own. If hiccups last an unusually long time or interfere with eating, sleeping or breathing, tell a trusted adult and seek medical advice.

What Did We Learn?

Hiccups happen when the diaphragm contracts involuntarily and the vocal cords close briefly. Several everyday activities can trigger this reflex.
